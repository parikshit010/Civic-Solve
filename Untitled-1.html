The problem gets reported. The report gets filed.

A pothole, a polluted stretch of river, a gap in disaster response — someone notices, someone files a complaint or writes a case study, and it mostly ends there. Meanwhile a university lab three kilometres away has the exact skills to help, and never hears about it.

On CivicSolve

The problem gets posted, matched, and worked.

The same problem goes up as a listing with a location, a domain, and a severity. Students, researchers, and industry teams who fit it are surfaced automatically, form a team in a shared workspace, and the sponsoring organisation can pilot what they build.

How it works

Eight steps carry a problem from a first report to a measured outcome.

1

A problem gets posted

Citizens, government bodies, or NGOs describe a real, local, or sector-wide problem worth solving.

2

It's sorted by domain, place, and stakes

Every listing is categorised by domain, location, severity, and expected impact so it can be found and prioritised.

3

Smart matching finds who should see it

An AI-based matching layer surfaces the universities, students, researchers, and industry partners best placed to work on it.

4

Interested teams form and collaborate

Matched contributors opt in and coordinate through a shared workspace built for the project.

5

Industry backs the strongest teams

Companies contribute funding, mentorship, datasets, or API access to the projects they believe in.

6

Teams build a solution

With support in place, the team develops and submits a working solution to the original problem.

7

A pilot gets run

Government bodies or the relevant organisation test promising solutions through a real pilot project.

8

Progress and impact are tracked

The platform follows each project from first post to pilot, recording outcomes and who benefited.

What's on the platform

Three core systems carry every project. Five more make the work fair, funded, and visible.

Problem repository

One place to post and browse societal challenges, searchable by domain, location, and how urgent they are.

AI smart matching

Matches each problem with the students, researchers, universities, and industry partners best suited to it.

Collaboration workspace

A shared space for matched teams to manage tasks, share documents, and communicate as they build.

Industry sponsorship

Companies fund and mentor the projects they want to see succeed.

Gamification

Points, badges, and recognition for contributors as projects progress.

Impact tracker

Follows a project from first report to pilot, and measures who it reached.

Geo-tagging

Problems carry a location, so local challenges reach local solvers.

Transparent contribution tracking

Records who contributed what, so credit and IP attribution stay clear.
